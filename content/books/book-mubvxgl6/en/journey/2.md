The people who hurt you are often the most accurate mirrors. The person who always irritates you, the one you keep trying to please, the old story you thought was long behind you — they're all still reflecting some corner of you. Looking in the mirror is not confessing to a crime. Many people hear "look inward" and immediately start blaming themselves, feeling like everything is their fault. That isn't looking in a mirror — that's hitting yourself with one. What you'll practice here is something different: seeing without attacking. Seeing where you're afraid, where you long to be validated, where you're handling new relationships with old tools. Many of the scripts still running in you today were learned years ago. They aren't wrong — they're expired. Seeing them clearly is the first step toward letting them retire.
