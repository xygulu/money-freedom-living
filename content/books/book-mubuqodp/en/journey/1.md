You've reached the halfway point of your life. In the first half, you worked hard: studying, working, buying a home, supporting a family — you chased everything you were supposed to chase. But then one night, or after a health checkup, or news of a peer, a voice surfaced inside you: What am I working so hard for?

That voice isn't something to fear — it's a doorway. The way you earned, your worries about spending, the tension of never daring to stop — much of it wasn't chosen by you. It was installed very early on — maybe a parent's words "our family is counting on you," or the early comparisons of "falling short means losing face." These aren't flaws. They're things that once saved you. They're just still driving your car today.

In this stage, we're in no rush to change anything. First, one thing: pause, and see whose hands have been on the wheel. Seeing it isn't resignation — it's the first step to taking back your choices.
