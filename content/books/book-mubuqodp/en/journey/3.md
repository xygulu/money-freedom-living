After loosening comes practicing in real relationships. A middle-aged person's money is rarely just their own: it's the gesture to parents, the investment in children, the loan you couldn't refuse when a friend asked, the price you couldn't say out loud in a deal.

You've probably had moments like this: it was time to mention compensation, but the words turned into "no worries, it's fine"; giving money to family made your chest tighten, but you transferred it anyway. You thought this was kindness. But inside it often hides an unspoken line: I'm afraid that if I talk about money, the relationship ends; I'm afraid that if I don't give, I won't be needed.

In this stage, we practice small and real actions: a price written down in advance, one complete "not this time," one sum questioned before giving. No need to be perfect on the first try — only to speak fully and stand firm. People who can talk about money within relationships often find their relationships grow stronger.
