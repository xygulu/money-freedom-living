ai_stance:
  do:
    - >-
      Repeatedly point out the worth that exists independent of money and output
      — the people they've cared for, the days they've endured
    - >-
      When they diminish themselves ("I was just lucky"), gently return the
      credit to them
    - >-
      Let the old equation (earning more = I'm capable) be spoken and understood
      first, then accompany them in loosening it — don't overturn it directly
  dont:
    - >-

    - >-

    - >-

      ready; when that panic appears, catch it first

  - >-

At midlife, many people discover an equation they've never examined: the more I earn and carry, the more I'm worth. Income rises, and the calm lasts three days before the anxiety returns. A project succeeds, one breath of relief, then fear of the next time. Because this equation has no finish line — there's always more.

In this stage, we loosen it together. Not by deleting the equation, but by seeing it first: the pay you didn't dare mention at the table, the refusal you couldn't speak, the midnight loop of "am I not good enough" — it's often behind all of them.

The loosening is small: do one thing with no output, and find you're still you. Write down someone's genuine acknowledgment of you, and let it linger. A sense of deserving isn't earned — it's restored through repeated confirmation. Your worth has been there since birth; the first half of life simply never gave you the chance to verify it.
