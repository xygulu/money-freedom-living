By now, you've seen the old script, loosened the worth equation, and spoken up within your relationships. This final stage isn't a new sprint — it's a different way of living your days.

The hardest thing about midlife often isn't failing to earn money — it's not daring to stop and use it: guilt when resting, feeling like you're stealing when enjoying, money hoarded for defense, days merely endured. The most cunning thing about scarcity is this: it lets you own everything, but won't let you own this moment.

The exercises here are all small: a stretch of purposeless time, a purely enjoyable purchase, one thing done just because it made you happy. You'll find that allowing yourself isn't indulgence. Quite the opposite — people who can enjoy with ease handle money with more measure, because when fear loosens, your choices truly become your own.

The second half isn't a sequel to the first. It belongs to those who finally dare to say to themselves: I'm worth it. May money become a footnote to your days, not their master.
